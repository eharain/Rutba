# Review of the end-to-end round — 2026-09-23

Four threads ran [e2e-two-instances.md](e2e-two-instances.md) on the dev
estate and wrote four records: [setup](E2E-2026-09-23-setup.md),
[organisation](E2E-2026-09-23-org.md), [individual](E2E-2026-09-23-individual.md)
and [parallel](E2E-2026-09-23-parallel.md). Four reviewers, one per record,
re-ran every read they could against the code and the live estate and wrote
their findings outside the repository; this record is the lead's synthesis.
Nothing in a code repository was changed by anyone in this round. Two things
the lead tried and could not do are recorded under Process: a one-row
confirmation write on the management database, and switching the dev
Strapi's mail to its log transport. Both were refused on this machine as
changes to a shared resource, and neither was worked around.

Headline: **the individual half of the programme is proven on a real
two-tenant core; the organisational half stopped at the first management
confirmation and is untested end to end.** Two tenants on one dev core answered
7,811 interleaved requests with no cross-tenant answer and no server error.
An individual registered by the API, did a day's work in Drive and Workspace,
drafted a Sign pack, shared, was isolated from a stranger, and was refused
every organisational door by mode. The operator opened the individual
instance through auth's internal handoff and did all five acts at the API.
But nothing that begins with a management account could be finished on this
estate: purchase, provisioning by the worker, the hub tile, the owner's
password, invitations, the colleague's bridge, and the individual's own bridge
from the hub. And no envelope can be sent on this estate at all, because the
core holds no seal key. Both walls are estate settings, not product code, and
each is one line away; the re-run they unlock is listed at the end.

## Findings that gate round three

Ordered by what they cost. Numbers continue from the round-two list, whose
six findings all stand.

7. **A management confirmation on this estate needs a database write or a
   setting change.** Management Strapi mails through SMTP; `rutba.test`
   bounces; the mailer has a log transport that prints the whole message,
   link included (`management/api/legacy/strapi/src/gates/mailer.js`, lines
   19 and 32 to 35, the default when `MAIL_TRANSPORT` is unset), but the dev
   file sets `smtp`. The organisational journey and the individual's hub step
   both stopped here; the parallel thread got through with the digest write
   on its own row. The organisation record's sentence that Strapi "has no log
   mode" is backwards; the wall is this estate's setting. Fix:
   `MAIL_TRANSPORT=log` on the dev Strapi, or the one-row statement in each
   record.
8. **No envelope can be sent on this estate.** `RUTBA_SIGN_SEAL_KEY` is read
   at `consumer/drive/api/sign/domain/seal.service.js` line 29, is set in no
   environment file, and nothing mints it: `management/devkit/scripts/gate-tokens.mjs`
   mints the licence and countersign keys and not this one. `smoke:sign`
   generates its own key, which is why 262 green checks never noticed. Both
   Sign ceremonies, the party's signing and the verify path went untested.
9. **A self-registered stranger can rewrite an organisation's shop.**
   `allow_register` is the users-permissions plugin's default, `true`; nothing
   in any repository writes it, and the only `false` is a test fixture.
   `pos_db` has it, `tpl_sign` was cloned from `pos_db`, so every organisation
   instance provisioned from the template arrives with an open door and
   `default_role: authenticated`. Registration then grants `storefront_user`
   (`consumer/console/api/auth/routes.js` lines 199 to 214), which holds 41
   write routes: CMS pages, footers, site settings, category groups, delivery
   zones and methods, customers. The same door still takes `app_roles` ids
   from the body (round two, finding 5). The organisation thread's fallback
   account also read the tenant's whole customer list, 41 rows with names and
   phones. Confirmed from the policy rows; nothing was exercised.
10. **The operator path is unusable from a browser, and round two was right.**
    The operator row is created on the `authenticated` users-permissions role
    (`consumer/console/api/auth/handoff.js` line 363) instead of
    `rutba_app_user`; the app's callback logs any other role type out and
    revokes the session (`packages/ui/components/AuthCallback.js` line 34,
    `packages/ui/context/AuthContext.js` line 518). The parallel thread proved
    the session is good at the API, with all five acts and their audit rows,
    and could not drive the browser hops; it concluded round two's consequence
    "did not hold". The reviewer read the callback: it does hold, for the only
    way staff would reach it. The seams row is corrected below. Fix, WS-A: one
    line, `type: 'rutba_app_user'`, plus a Sign policy for the operator.
11. **The stale individual record makes `pos_db` bridgeable.** The reconcile
    finds its row by `tenantRef` (`management/api/legacy/strapi/src/estate/individual.js`
    lines 186 to 189), so moving `INDIVIDUAL_INSTANCE_DB` left id 47 (`pos_db`,
    descriptor `mode: individual`) active beside id 106. The setup record said
    a holder of 47 could open an operator session on the organisation; it
    cannot, because `resolveForOperate` refuses outside individual mode
    (`handoff.js` lines 346 to 349). The real hole is worse: `listBridged`
    (`estate/bridge.js` lines 124 to 131) offers both rows to operators, and
    the `open` purpose (`handoff.js` lines 245 to 263) has no mode check, so
    anyone holding the internal key can bind a management subject onto any
    confirmed `pos_db` row and get a session, which is round two's finding 1
    with a second target. The one-row retirement is still unapplied.
12. **An individual database is seeded the whole organisational catalogue.**
    `individual_dev` holds 160 app roles, 154 of them organisational
    (`_admin` 49, `_manager` 49, `_staff` 47, 9 others), not the 47 the setup
    record counted. The cause is not a seeder that never prunes:
    `roleBelongsInMode` (`consumer/api/core/src/policy/individual.js` lines 81
    to 86) keeps every non-individual key in both modes, so a prune or a
    per-mode template would be seeded back on the next boot. Decision 4 (role
    sets exclusive by mode) is enforced at the catalogue level in one
    direction only. Nothing grants them today; the individual register door
    refuses a body that names roles.
13. **The template carries the source tenant's secrets.** All 317 core-store
    rows travelled `pos_db` to `tpl_sign` to `individual_dev`; `core_admin_auth`
    and the users-permissions grant hash identically in all three. Round two's
    "the template copies the core store" was met live and the setup record did
    not record it. The tenancy doc blesses copying "the store settings" while
    promising the template "carries no instance's own platform keys"; both are
    true and together they leak the admin secret across tenants.
14. **Withdrawn: the consumer auth app renders; the hidden pane did not.**
    Three records blamed the browser pane, the parallel reviewer measured
    against it, and the individual re-run settled it the other way: a hidden
    pane never fires `requestAnimationFrame`, and Next's development client
    waits for a frame before it hydrates (`next/dist/client/page-bootstrap.js`
    line 29 hands off to its flash-of-unstyled-content guard). One screenshot
    makes the pane draw a frame and the page hydrates at once, on the 4003
    sign-in page, the Sign app's 404 page and its ceremony page alike.
    `router.isReady` reads true whether or not the page hydrated, so it never
    decided anything. Production's sign-in and launcher settle for a signed-out
    visitor (addendum below). What stands from this item is finding 15's
    503 on a token-less setup-state read. The rule in README.md is amended.
15. **Nothing in dev names a tenant without a token.** A token-less request
    needs `X-Rutba-Domain` and `X-Rutba-Edge-Key`; no app originates them and
    dev has no edge. Individual registration is API-only, and the switch also
    took the dev storefront's own sign-in and sign-up out of service
    (`consumer/packages/api-client/api/web/auth.js` lines 4 and 5 post
    token-less by domain). Two records wrote that no consumer app posts to the
    register route; the storefront does (`content/apps/storefront/src/services/auth.ts`
    line 19). The auth app has no registration screen in any mode.

16. **The app shell keys on the users-permissions role type, so every
    self-registered organisational account is locked out by construction.**
    The launcher's gate is the role type (`consumer/console/apps/auth/pages/login.js`
    line 235): a person registered through the open door lands on
    `authenticated`, the token door mints for them, and the launcher then
    refuses "Your account does not have the required role". Same mechanism as
    finding 10, seen from the other side; together they say the shell's
    identity of an app user is the role type, and two doors create people
    without it.

Three more of note, lower: the instance mail in log mode prints recipient and
subject and never the link (`consumer/api/platform/src/email.js` line 113; the
tenants-door smoke asserts that same one-line shape), so a set-password or a
ceremony link is unrecoverable from any log; the Sign app's verify page fails
for a signed-out visitor because it fetches a public endpoint through the
authenticated client (`providers/generated/client/sign/sign-envelopes.js` line
357 into `lib/api.js` line 1057, while the no-auth client sits at line 810), a
one-line fix; and `bridge.instance_handoff` records no caller
(`management/auth/src/http/routes/internal.routes.js` lines 126 to 131) while
the minted-token event does, so the audit cannot tell a console-minted
operator session from one the internal key minted elsewhere.

## Process

What worked: the gate line, the briefs read before the first step, records
committed midway, and reviewers who re-ran reads rather than re-reading
prose; every count that a reviewer could reproduce did.

What did not:

- **Three of four threads met the same wall and three sessions were treated
  differently by the permission guard.** The parallel thread's confirmation
  write on its own row was allowed; the individual and organisation threads
  were refused the identical statement; the lead was refused it and the
  mail-transport switch too. A round that depends on a management
  confirmation needs that decided before it starts: log mail on the dev
  Strapi, or the owner's rule allowing the write.
- **The estate profile.** `erp` carries neither the portal site, the portal
  console nor the management console, so the organisation's browser steps and
  the operator's page had nothing to open. The next browser round runs a
  profile that has them.
- **No screenshots and no timestamps** on tables read from databases that
  three threads were writing to; one quota snapshot had drifted by the time
  its reviewer read it.
- **The gateway log buffer is 199 lines.** Any claim about a window longer
  than a minute has to come from the runner's own census, and the records
  should say so.
- **Records contradicted each other** on whether a consumer app registers
  people, and two records retracted or asserted a browser cause without the
  two-line hydration check that decides it.
- **The restore section was written before the change and never rehearsed.**

Rules added to README.md: a claim about a browser names the hydration check
that backs it; a table read from a shared database carries the minute it was
read; a restore is rehearsed, and it hashes the destinations.
